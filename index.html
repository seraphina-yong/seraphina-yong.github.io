Seraphina Yong

Postdoctoral Fellow at SFU SIAT

seraphina_yong @sfu.ca

Seraphina is a Canada Impact+ Research Training Awards (CIRTA) fellow in the School of Interactive Arts and Technology (SIAT) at Simon Fraser University. She is currently working with Dr. Alissa Antle to design embodied narrative experiences for parent-child emotional co-regulation. Seraphina received her Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Minnesota, advised by Dr. Lana Yarosh and Dr. Evan Suma Rosenberg.

Seraphina designs embodied experiences that support interpersonal bonding at a phenomenological level, targeting critical and emerging contexts such as technology-mediated friendship, intersubjectivity through virtual embodiment, and generative AI in close relationships. Her work lies at the intersection of Human-Computer Interaction, Well-Being, and Neuroaesthetics. Her research approach draws on the connections between playful, emotionally-evocative experiences and the achievement of important functional social goals such as conflict resolution and relationship maintenance.

Her interests outside of research include playing and listening to music, reading manga, and going on nature walks.

Recent Projects

Neurofeedback-Driven Embodied Narrative Interfaces for Parent-Child Emotional Co-Regulation

Collaborator: Alissa Antle

All parents and their children face emotion regulation issues, especially during middle childhood. Customary social practices shift the sole responsibility to the parent, although both parties are often struggling in such scenarios. We propose that enabling parents and children to work together in co-regulating their emotions during times of stress can not only support more effective regulation but also improve parent-child bonds.

What Generative AI is Doing to Teens' Friendships, Sense of Connection, and Identity

Main Collaborators: Lana Yarosh, Mindy Zhao, and Anna Martin-Boyle

Generative AI can provide valuable support to challenging interpersonal interactions as third parties, but ethical issues such as overreliance threaten individuals' sense of relationship authenticity, which heavily impacts personally significant relationships such as friendship. We design and evaluate open-ended linguistic stylizations of AI generated conversation summaries (e.g., as poems) to encourage critical discourse during difficult conversations.
